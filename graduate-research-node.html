OBJECTIVE

Examine whether specific usability features in Minecraft: Creative Mode were associated with players' experiences of flow, immersion, and an altered perception of time. (N=117 adult participants; mixed-methods design.)

AUDIOVISUAL IMPACT
  • In-Game Sounds (90%+ Immersion Enh.)
  • Background Music & Ambient Audio
  • Menu Design Cohesion
FLOW FACILITATORS
  • Hotbar Clarity & UI Design
  • Inventory Ease (r=0.74 w/ Flow)
  • Social Chat Feature (r=0.71 Cross-Construct)
TEMPORAL DISTORTION
  • Building & Creative Tasks (Strongest Overall)
  • Inventory Loss of Time (r=0.51, Flow)
  • World Modification & Appearance (r=0.47, Immersion)
DESIGN IMPLICATIONS
  • Holistic Usability: Audio and social features are core to immersion, not just secondary.
  • Cognitive Load: Intuitive UI reduces friction, allowing faster entry into flow states.
  • Creative Loops: Building tasks are the primary drivers of altered time perception.
FUTURE RESEARCH
  • Genre Expansion: Replication in other sandbox games (Roblox, Terraria) and broader game genres.
  • Causal Mapping: Longitudinal and experimental designs to establish causal rather than correlational relationships.
  • Demographic Differences: Examining how age, gender, and gaming experience shape these relationships.

FINDINGS SUMMARY

> DISSERTATION RESULTS:

Findings suggest that game usability extends beyond traditional definitions to include audiovisual design, social connectivity, interface clarity, satisfaction-based features, and creative tasks. When well-designed, these features showed meaningful associations with deeper engagement, flow, and immersion. Flow and immersion demonstrated a strong bidirectional relationship with each other (r=0.56–0.71), forming a positive feedback loop where each state reinforces the other. Both were positively associated with temporal distortion, and all 117 participants reported at least some degree of altered time perception, suggesting that even minimal engagement may be sufficient to contribute to this effect.
